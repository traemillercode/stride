Best daily trainers for new runners, summer 2026

Five shoes we put through 200+ miles of road, sidewalk, and treadmill testing. Here's what earned a spot in our rotation — and which one belongs in yours.

Your first pair of real running shoes is a commitment — not just of money, but of trust. These are the shoes that will carry you through early-morning starts, rainy weekday miles, and the long runs where you discover what you're capable of. The daily trainer category is the workhorse of any runner's rotation: durable enough to log hundreds of miles, cushioned enough to protect your legs, and versatile enough to handle everything from recovery jogs to tempo efforts.

We tested twelve shoes across six weeks. Five earned a spot on this list. Here's what we found.

Nike Pegasus — $130

The Pegasus is the all-around workhorse that's been a staple of running store walls for decades — and for good reason. The latest version pairs a responsive Zoom Air unit in the forefoot with a React foam midsole that strikes the right balance between cushion and ground feel. It's not the softest shoe on this list, and it's not the lightest, but it's the one that disappears on your foot on day one. For new runners who want one shoe that can do everything from a 5K to a 10-miler without complaint, the Pegasus is still the safest bet in the category.

Brooks Ghost 16 — $140

If the Ghost were a car, it'd be a Honda Accord: not flashy, but it'll run forever. Brooks's DNA Loft v3 cushioning gives the Ghost 16 a smooth, consistent ride that doesn't bottom out on long miles or feel mushy on shorter ones. The upper is generous in the toe box without being sloppy, and the heel counter locks the foot in without pressure points. Our heavier testers (180-200 lbs) rated this shoe highest for all-day comfort, and it showed almost no wear after 50 miles. The trade-off: it's not inspiring at faster paces. But for the easy and moderate miles where most new runners live, it's hard to beat.

Saucony Ride 18 — $140

The Ride 18 surprised us. Saucony's PWRRUN+ foam delivers a balanced ride that's firmer than the Ghost but more energetic than the Pegasus — a Goldilocks middle ground that testers kept coming back to. The upper breathes well on hot days and the outsole rubber coverage is generous, which matters when you're still learning to land cleanly. This was the shoe our lighter testers (120-150 lbs) gravitated toward for their longer efforts. If you want a shoe that stays out of your way and lets you focus on the run, the Ride 18 deserves a spot on your shortlist.

Hoka Clifton 10 — $145

The Clifton 10 packs maximum cushion into a surprisingly light package, and Hoka's meta-rocker geometry gives the shoe a rolling-forward sensation that makes easy paces feel effortless. The stack height looks intimidating, but the wide base keeps the shoe stable even when your form starts to fade late in a run. Our testers recovering from shin splints and knee niggles consistently rated the Clifton highest for comfort and joint relief. The trade-off: some runners found the rocker geometry took a few runs to get used to, and the softer foam isn't as durable on abrasive pavement. If comfort is your top priority — and for most new runners, it should be — the Clifton 10 is the one to beat.

ASICS Gel-Nimbus 27 — $160

The Gel-Nimbus 27 is the plush cruiser of the group — the shoe you reach for when the plan says 10 miles and your legs are asking for mercy. ASICS combines their PureGEL technology in the heel with a full-length FF BLAST+ midsole that's noticeably softer than anything else on this list. The engineered knit upper wraps the foot like a sock, and the outsole has enough rubber to handle wet sidewalks without slipping. At $160 it's the most expensive shoe here, and it's heavier than the Clifton or Ride. But for runners who want maximum protection on long miles — or who simply want their feet to feel coddled — the premium is worth it.

How we tested

We didn't just unbox these shoes and jog around the block. Our test protocol put 200+ miles across five testers over six weeks — a mix of road, sidewalk, and treadmill surfaces. Testers ranged from 120 to 200 pounds, with running experience from first-season beginners to multi-year marathoners. Every shoe logged at least 30 miles before we formed an opinion, and we tracked comfort, durability, ride quality, and value independently before comparing notes.

We paid particular attention to how each shoe held up in the final two weeks of testing, when midsole foam tends to break down and upper materials start showing real wear. Shoes that lost their character before 50 miles were cut.

The verdict

Best all-around: Nike Pegasus. If you buy one shoe, make it this one. It does everything well enough that you'll never regret it.

Best comfort: ASICS Gel-Nimbus 27. The priciest option, but the plush ride is unmatched — especially for runners over 170 lbs or anyone logging double-digit miles weekly.

Best value: Saucony Ride 18. At $140 with PWRRUN+ foam and excellent durability, this is the shoe that punches above its weight class.

Best for comfort-first runners: Hoka Clifton 10. Maximum cushion, minimum weight — ideal if joint pain or impact sensitivity are concerns.

Most reliable: Brooks Ghost 16. Not exciting, but it'll outlast everything else and your feet will thank you at mile 8 of your first long run.

The best daily trainer is the one you'll actually run in. Go to a running store, try on at least three of these, and jog around the block in each one. Fit is personal. What feels right on your foot matters more than any review — including this one.
