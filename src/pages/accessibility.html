Accessibility

How Stride works for every runner, on every device

Stride is committed to making our platform accessible to every runner. We target WCAG 2.1 Level AAA — the highest standard for web accessibility — and we build with that goal from day one. This is not a compliance checkbox for us. It's part of building a community that truly includes everyone, regardless of how they access the web.

Where we are today

We're early in our journey and we want to be honest about that. Here's what we have in place and what we're still working on.

What we do well

  • AAA color contrast. We target 7:1 contrast for body text and 4.5:1 for large text — stricter than the AA minimum most sites use. Our dark charcoal text against off-white surfaces is built for readability.
  • Semantic HTML. Pages use proper heading hierarchies, landmark regions, and meaningful document structure so screen readers can navigate efficiently.
  • ARIA labels on interactive elements. Buttons, links, and form controls carry descriptive labels. Our inline SVG icons include accessible attributes so they're not invisible to assistive technology.
  • Keyboard navigation. Every page is fully navigable by keyboard alone. Focus states use visible cobalt-blue outlines so you always know where you are.
  • Readable type. Our type system uses generous sizing, comfortable line heights, and a clean sans-serif body font designed for extended reading.
  • Motion sensitivity. We avoid auto-playing animations and respect the prefers-reduced-motion media query across our interface.
  • Responsive layout. The site works across screen sizes without horizontal scrolling or content cut-off.

Known limitations

We're a small team building in the open, and there are areas we know need work. Here's what we're tracking:

  • Dynamic plan content. The roadmap builder inserts content into the page after load. We're improving how screen readers are notified of these live region updates.
  • Inline SVG icons. While we use accessible attributes, some decorative icons still need improved labeling — particularly those that sit alongside visible text labels.
  • Community feed updates. Real-time content refreshes (new posts, pace-space messages) don't yet provide consistent screen reader announcements.
  • Pace calculator tables. The results table uses visual styling to highlight rows. We're adding programmatic cues so the highlight meaning is conveyed non-visually.
  • Onboarding stepper. Multi-step onboarding uses a stepper pattern — step transitions need improved focus management for keyboard and screen reader users.
  • Third-party embeds. Future integrations (Spotify, YouTube, Apple Music) will bring embedded players with their own accessibility characteristics. We'll evaluate each embed partner's accessibility before integrating.

These are tracked internally and we address the highest-impact items first. If you encounter something we haven't listed, please let us know.

How we test

Accessibility testing is part of our build process, not an afterthought. We use a combination of:

  • Manual review. Every new page and feature is manually reviewed against WCAG 2.1 AAA criteria by a team member before it ships.
  • Automated contrast checking. We test every color pairing for AAA contrast ratios throughout development.
  • Screen reader testing. We navigate the full site with VoiceOver (macOS) and NVDA (Windows) to catch issues that automated tools miss.
  • Keyboard-only testing. We run through every interaction using only the keyboard — Tab, Enter, Escape, arrow keys — to ensure nothing is mouse-dependent.
  • Zoom and text scaling. Pages are tested at 200% zoom and with browser text scaling to verify no content or function is lost.

Report an accessibility issue

If you encounter a barrier on Stride, we want to hear about it. Accessibility issues get our immediate attention — we treat them as high-priority bugs, not feature requests.

Email us with the subject line "Accessibility Report" and describe what you encountered — which page, what you were trying to do, and what browser or assistive technology you're using. Screenshots help but aren't required.

accessibility@stride.com

Subject line: Accessibility Report

We aim to acknowledge every report within 3 business days and provide a timeline for resolution within 10 business days.

Feedback is always welcome

You don't need to hit a barrier to reach out. If something feels awkward, inconsistent, or harder than it should be — even if it technically works — tell us. Accessibility isn't just about meeting a spec. It's about the real experience.

For general feedback (not specifically about accessibility), reach us through our support contact.

Conformance note

This statement is a living document. As we close gaps and improve, this page will reflect our current status — not a static snapshot from launch week. Full AAA conformance is our target, but we won't claim it until we've verified every page against every criterion and resolved all known issues.
