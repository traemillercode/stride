Health & fitness data disclosure

What body-adjacent data Stride collects and how it's used

Stride is not a medical provider.

Training plans, pace calculations, mileage targets, injury risk assessments, nutrition guidance, and all other content on Stride are for informational and community purposes only. They are not medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment.

Consult a qualified physician or healthcare provider before beginning any training program, modifying your exercise routine, or acting on any fitness-related information you find on this platform. Running and endurance training carry inherent risk of injury. You assume full responsibility for your training decisions and physical activity.

What body-adjacent data Stride collects

When you use Stride — and especially when you connect a device — we may collect the following health- and fitness-related data points:

  • Body weight. Self-reported during onboarding or pulled from connected device platforms.
  • Mileage and distance. Weekly and per-run mileage from connected devices or manual entry.
  • Injury history. Self-reported during onboarding — the "easy-run pace" and injury background fields in our plan builder.
  • Pace data. Current easy pace, race paces, and historical pace trends from connected devices.
  • Heart rate. Resting heart rate, maximum heart rate, and workout heart rate data when available from Garmin, Apple Health, or other connected platforms.
  • Training load. Calculated weekly load, intensity distribution, and training stress derived from your activity data.
  • Race results. Past race times and distances — both imported from devices and self-reported.
  • Cadence, elevation, and GPS routes. Detailed workout-level metrics when provided by connected devices.

How this data is used

We use fitness and health data for specific platform features — never for advertising or profiling beyond what powers the tools you've chosen to use:

  • Roadmap and training plan construction. Your pace, mileage history, injury background, and race goals inform the periodized plans our platform builds. The plan builder's mileage-safety logic — which caps weekly increases and flags potential overtraining — uses your historical data to keep recommendations within safe ranges.
  • Pace predictions. Our pace calculator uses established methodologies (Riegel, VDOT) to project race finish times based on your actual training data. These are estimates, not guarantees.
  • Injury risk flagging. The platform compares your recent training load against your historical baseline and flags unusually sharp increases. This is a statistical signal, not a medical assessment. If the platform flags a spike, that's a cue to check in with yourself — not a diagnosis.
  • Community sharing. Training metrics you choose to share (pace, mileage, race results) appear in community spaces, activity cards, and shared pace groups. You control what's visible.
  • Race field projections. Aggregated pace and distance data from users registered for the same race helps power our field-projection tool, which estimates where you might finish relative to the field.

You control what you share

  • Device connections are opt-in. Stride never pulls data from a device platform without your explicit authorization. You connect Garmin, Strava, Coros, Apple Health, Amazfit, or Suunto through each platform's official OAuth flow, and you can revoke access at any time from your account settings or the device platform's app management page.
  • Community posts are user-controlled. You decide what training data appears in community spaces. Pace groups, activity cards, and shared routes are opt-in features — you choose to join a pace group; you choose which activities to post.
  • Auto-post is off by default. Stride will offer an opt-in auto-share feature that posts completed workouts to your community feed automatically. This is explicitly opt-in, off by default, and each post is marked with its data source (e.g., "via Garmin").
  • Data deletion. You can request full deletion of your data at any time. See our Data Deletion page for instructions.

Data we do not collect

To be explicit: Stride does not collect or process medical records, blood work, genetic data, sleep data (unless provided by a connected device as part of its standard activity sync), menstrual cycle data, or any data classified as protected health information (PHI) under HIPAA. Stride is not a HIPAA-covered entity.

Device partners and their policies

Remember: Stride is a training companion, not a coach or clinician.

The plans, numbers, and flags this platform produces are tools to support your training — they are not personalized medical or professional coaching advice. Listen to your body. If something hurts or doesn't feel right, stop and consult a qualified professional.
